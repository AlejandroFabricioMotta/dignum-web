From 20 to 80 chats a day: what about the people?

October 29, 2026 · 5-minute read

A store went from 20 to more than 80 daily chats without hiring more staff. A huge number. But no one says what happened to the person who handles them.

Automation numbers always talk about sales. They almost never talk about who carries what the automation doesn't solve.

Tienda Arlequín, a gift business in Catamarca, installed an automatic assistant on WhatsApp. According to NeuralActions, a developer that Dignum represents internationally, in 30 days conversions rose 42%, automatic 24/7 replies grew 65%, and response time fell 30%. They went from handling 20 chats a day to more than 80, without adding staff.

It is a strong result. But there is a question those numbers don't answer: What happened to the person who used to handle those 20 chats?

There are three possible paths when a tool multiplies demand:

  • They get overloaded. The assistant answers the easy stuff, but the hard stuff (complaints, doubts, big sales) reaches the same person, now with four times the volume.
  • They get replaced. The job disappears, and with it the experience they had.
  • Their job changes, with a plan. They are trained, their role is redefined, and they take the conversations where their judgment is worth the most.

The phrase “without hiring more staff” can be read as an achievement or as a warning. In a small business, growing without breaking the bank is good news. The risk appears when “without hiring” ends up meaning “without caring.”

Three questions for any implementation:

  1. Who handles what the assistant doesn't resolve, and how much time does it take?
  2. What part of the freed-up time goes back to the person, in training or in better conversations?
  3. Did anyone ask them how they are experiencing the change?

At Dignum we believe no automation should be switched on without a transition plan, and that technology must complement people, not compete with them.

Put it into practice: The last time a tool saved you time at your company, did that time turn into more workload, a cutback, or something better?
