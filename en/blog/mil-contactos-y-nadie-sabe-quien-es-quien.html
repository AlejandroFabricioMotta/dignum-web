More than a thousand contacts and nobody knows who is who

November 9, 2026 · 5-minute read

A messy database is not a technical problem. It is people receiving the wrong message, or none.

Before thinking about artificial intelligence to sell more, it's worth asking something more basic: do we know who we are talking to?

Almost every organization lives the same scene. There are contacts in the CRM, others in a spreadsheet, others in the website form, and others in billing. The same person appears two or three times, with a misspelled last name or no email. Nobody decided it that way: it happened over time.

The cost shows up in daily life. You write to the same customer twice. You offer a course they already took. Someone gets no reply because their inquiry ended up in a spreadsheet nobody opened. And behind it, a team member entering data by hand, feeling it never ends.

In the case of the RGM Global Academy, a database of more than a thousand records, with incomplete and duplicate data, was organized with an AI agent. It corrects and completes data, detects duplicates, tags each contact by profile and interests, and syncs with the web. It also makes weekly backups.

What matters is what the agent does not do: it doesn't decide alone in sensitive cases. A person approves that. The team's work shifts: less time entering data, more time talking with people.

Before organizing your data, it's worth settling three decisions:

  1. Which is the source of truth when two systems don't match.
  2. Who approves doubtful corrections and merges, by name.
  3. What copy is saved before each change, so you can go back.

Put it into practice: If you had to write to a customer today, would you know for certain who they are, what interests them, and what they were told last time?
