Five questions to ask your AI provider about your people

October 29, 2026 · 5-minute read

Before buying AI, almost everyone asks how much it costs and what it saves. Almost no one asks what happens to their people. These are the five questions that can't be missing.

A serious artificial intelligence provider must be able to answer five questions about the people in your company. If it can't, it isn't ready yet.

When a company decides to bring in AI, the conversation with the provider usually revolves around technology, price, and savings. The people who will live with it every day are rarely discussed. These five questions help change that conversation.

  1. What will the AI do and what won't it? Ask for the limits in writing: which tasks it takes on, which decisions it never makes, and what information it cannot use.
  2. Who answers when the AI fails or doesn't know? There must be a named person who supervises and to whom you can turn. Human oversight, always.
  3. What happens to the people whose work changes? The answer must include a transition plan: reassignment, retraining, or upskilling. And it must exist before the tool is switched on, not after.
  4. What data does it use and where does it end up? Customer, employee, and company data. Confidentiality and security by design, not as an add-on.
  5. How will we know, six months from now, whether it worked for the people? Not only whether it sold more or cost less. Also what the people who work with the tool say, including the criticism.

How to use this list. Send the list to the provider before signing and ask for the answers in writing. A vague answer to question 3 is, in itself, an answer.
