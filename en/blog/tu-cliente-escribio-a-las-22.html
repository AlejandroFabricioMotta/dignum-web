Your customer wrote at 10:00 PM and nobody answered

October 29, 2026 · 5-minute read

An unanswered inquiry is usually a lost sale. But answering fast is not enough if no one decides who handles the rest.

Answering fast is already the floor, not a differentiator. What makes the difference is what happens to the people behind it.

Think about the last time you wrote to a business after hours. If nobody answered, you probably bought somewhere else. On the other side, nobody decided to lose that sale: there was simply no one to reply.

According to NeuralActions, a solutions development company represented internationally by Dignum, it has developed a WhatsApp assistant to address this need.

The problem has four faces: sales lost by not answering instantly, frequent inquiries left unattended in physical stores, purchases abandoned because of the delay of a human reply, and lack of follow-up after the sale.

The proposed solution is an artificial intelligence assistant on WhatsApp, the channel where customers already are. It instantly answers the repetitive (stock, sizes, catalog, hours), advises, helps close the order, and reminds the customer in the next conversation.

So far, the usual story. What is almost never told is who handles what the assistant can't resolve. A well-designed assistant answers the repetitive and hands to a person what calls for judgment: a complaint, a negotiation, a customer who needs to feel heard. That person doesn't disappear. Their work changes: fewer repeated questions, more conversations that matter.

For it to work, three decisions are needed before switching it on:

  1. What the assistant answers and what it doesn't, in writing.
  2. Who the conversation goes to when it doesn't know, by name, not “the team”.
  3. How that person is trained before day one, not after.

Put it into practice: If tomorrow an assistant answered all the repetitive inquiries of your business, what would you let your team do with that time?

Source: CX NeuralActions, accessed on October 29, 2026.
